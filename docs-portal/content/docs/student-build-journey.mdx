NetPredict was born in a university lab out of deep frustration with academic "AI in networking". In textbooks and coursework, network ML is often reduced to a toy classification task: take a static synthetic CSV, shuffle rows into a random 80/20 train-test split, fit a decision tree, and boast about 99% accuracy. In the real world, that model is dead on arrival.

Real-world production networks are dynamic, kinetic physical systems. High-speed switch queues do not fail instantaneously; they accumulate microbursts over minutes, exhibiting subtle round-trip time slopes (ΔRTT/Δt), queue bufferbloat, and violent packet drop cliffs when tail-drop buffers saturate. NetPredict was engineered from scratch to move beyond reactive alarms into forward-looking, physics-grounded telemetry forecasting (T+5m, T+15m, T+30m) with distribution-free uncertainty guarantees and sub-2ms execution.

### The Engineering Journey: 6 Core Breakthroughs

Building an enterprise-grade telemetry forecaster as an undergraduate required confronting deep mathematical and systems challenges:

#### 1. Why LSTMs Failed and Multi-Horizon LightGBM Won
We initially built sequence deep learning models (2-layer LSTMs and GRUs). In theory, recurrent hidden states should capture temporal patterns. In practice, they were an operational failure. Microbursts (200ms–2s) cause abrupt phase shifts; recurrent gates smoothed over sharp transients, suffered severe gradient attenuation, demanded GPU accelerators, and took 75ms+ per inference step. We pivoted to **Multi-Horizon LightGBM**. By pairing 18 causal rolling statistical features (EWMA, rolling std, delay slopes) with histogram binning, LightGBM isolates discontinuous cliff edges in under **1.8ms on a single CPU core** with zero GPU dependencies.

#### 2. The Battle Against Temporal Data Leakage
Our initial prototype achieved 99.4% ROC-AUC on standard 5-fold cross-validation. It looked like a triumph until replay testing collapsed. The cause was **temporal autocorrelation leakage**: random splitting allowed past folds to peek into future autocorrelation. We instituted **Strict Walk-Forward Chronological Validation** with a mandatory **embargo gap** (g >= 30m) between train and test splits, backed by an in-memory ring buffer (`collections.deque(maxlen=720)`) that enforces backward-only windowing. Our honest hold-out test metrics: **ROC-AUC 0.8389, PR-AUC 0.6125**.

#### 3. Probability Calibration (Brier Score 0.0157)
Raw gradient-boosted decision trees output uncalibrated leaf logits. If a NOC screen flashes "80% failure risk", exactly 80 out of 100 such predictions must result in actual degradation; otherwise operators suffer alert fatigue and mute alarms. We fitted a non-parametric **Isotonic Regression** (PAVA) model on out-of-fold validation scores:

```text
Minimize sum( (y_i - p_hat_i)^2 ) subject to p_hat_i <= p_hat_j for x_i <= x_j
```

This compressed our Brier score from **0.0482 to 0.0157**, transforming raw tree outputs into dependable operational probabilities.

#### 4. Split Conformal Prediction (90% Finite-Sample Coverage)
Point predictions are irresponsible during volatile traffic spikes. We implemented **Split Conformal Prediction** to wrap telemetry forecasts in rigorous, distribution-free prediction intervals:

```text
Interval: C(X) = [ y_pred(X) - q_hat,  y_pred(X) + q_hat ]
Coverage: P( Y in C(X) ) >= 1 - alpha
```

By computing absolute residual non-conformity scores `s_i = |y_i - y_pred(X_i)|` on a calibration split, we extract the `(1 - alpha)(1 + 1/n)`-th empirical quantile `q_hat`. NetPredict guarantees **90% coverage (alpha = 0.10)** regardless of underlying traffic distribution shifts.

#### 5. Real-Time TreeSHAP Exact Attributions (< 2ms)
Network engineers will not trust a black box that predicts failure without proof. Instead of slow model-agnostic perturbations (`KernelSHAP O(2^M)`), we implemented Lundberg's polynomial-time **TreeSHAP algorithm `O(T * L * D^2)`**. It computes exact additive Shapley values across all ensemble paths in **< 1.8ms**, synthesized directly into natural language diagnostics (e.g., *"Queue delay slope contributed +42% to risk"*).

#### 6. Counterfactual Decision Simulation
Predicting failure without a mitigation path leaves operators helpless. We built the **What-If Scenario Lab** to simulate physical interventions before touching edge routers:

```text
X*_util  = X0_util  * (1 - reroute_pct / 100) * (1 - rate_limit_pct / 100)
X*_queue = (X0_queue / buffer_factor) * (1 - reroute_pct / 100)
```

The modified vector `X*` is passed through calibrated LightGBM to classify intervention efficacy (**OPTIMAL**, **EFFECTIVE**, or **INEFFECTIVE**) in real time.

### Operator Walkthrough: The 7 Cockpit Sections

The NetPredict cockpit is built as a high-density tactical interface organized into 7 operational sections:

<Callout type="info">
Every section is navigable via the sidebar or top stream controls (`Step`, `Jump to Incident`, `Auto-Play`, `Reset`).
</Callout>

1. **Section 01: Live Telemetry Grid**
   Displays real-time dials for Packet Loss %, Round-Trip Time (ms), Jitter, Interface Utilization %, and Buffer Occupancy %. Color-coded thresholds immediately highlight metric degradation.
2. **Section 02: Predictive Horizons Panel**
   Shows forward risk across three distinct operational horizons:
   * **T+5m**: Microburst and buffer drop cliff detection.
   * **T+15m**: Primary tactical window for automated traffic rerouting.
   * **T+30m**: Strategic window for cascading congestion prevention.
   Each horizon incorporates **Split Conformal Prediction intervals** expressing calibrated uncertainty bounds.
3. **Section 03: Anomaly vs Prediction Card**
   Visualizes the decoupled 4-quadrant operational matrix:
   * *Quadrant 1 (Nominal)*: Normal operation.
   * *Quadrant 2 (Early Warning)*: Anomaly `FALSE`, Prediction `HIGH RISK` — the ultimate proactive window.
   * *Quadrant 3 (Transient Outlier)*: Anomaly `TRUE`, Prediction `LOW RISK` — suppresses false alarms.
   * *Quadrant 4 (Critical Cascade)*: Anomaly `TRUE`, Prediction `HIGH RISK` — triggers immediate failover.
4. **Section 04: Root-Cause Explainer (TreeSHAP)**
   Renders a real-time waterfall chart decomposing the current prediction into positive and negative metric contributions, allowing operators to verify root causes in milliseconds.
5. **Section 05: What-If Scenario Lab**
   Provides interactive sliders for candidate mitigations:
   * *Traffic Diversion %* (0–100%): Reroute ingress flows over alternative paths.
   * *Rate Limiting %* (0–90%): Clamp upstream burst rate.
   * *Buffer Expansion Factor* (1.0x–3.0x): Dynamically allocate burst buffer memory.
   Operators preview risk reduction and get a deterministic verdict before touching switch configurations.
6. **Section 06: Model Health & Audit**
   Monitors model trustworthiness in production: Brier calibration score (0.0157), reliability curves, KS-drift metrics, and sub-2ms inference latency audit logs.
7. **Section 07: Incident History Table**
   Provides a chronological audit log of historical incidents, recording event timestamps, peak risk scores, root-cause tags, and mitigation outcomes for post-mortem analysis.

### Why Tier-1 Infrastructure Teams Care

What makes NetPredict an engineering marvel rather than a student class project?

* **Sub-2ms Deterministic Execution**: By rejecting heavy neural networks in favor of optimized LightGBM and polynomial TreeSHAP, NetPredict runs on standard edge CPU cores without expensive GPU clusters in remote Points of Presence (PoPs).
* **Mathematical Coverage Guarantees**: Through Split Conformal Prediction, operators receive distribution-free 90% confidence bounds, removing guesswork during traffic volatility.
* **Elimination of Alert Fatigue**: With an Isotonic Brier score of 0.0157 and decoupled anomaly detection, operators are never paged for harmless transient spikes.
* **Closed-Loop Actionability**: The counterfactual simulation engine bridges predictive telemetry with software-defined networking (SDN) controllers, enabling automated, validated mitigations.
